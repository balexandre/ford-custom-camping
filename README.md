# Transit Custom Camp Kit

Plans for turning a Ford Transit Custom Kombi with a wheelchair lift into a weekend camper, without taking away what makes it wheelchair-accessible.

**Open the planner: https://balexandre.github.io/ford-custom-camping/**

## The van

- Ford Transit Custom **Kombi** (the passenger version) with the **high roof**, built in 2023 and converted for wheelchair users.
- A two-pillar **wheelchair lift** (rated 400 kg) folds up inside the rear doors, so the back of the van can't be used for loading or for a kitchen.
- **Two pairs of floor rails** run the length of the floor: one pair under the seats along the left wall, and one pair down the middle for strapping in a wheelchair.
- The **two seats along the left wall** sit on quick-release bases in the rails, so they come out for camping.
- The **seat on the right**, behind the sliding door, is fixed and can't be removed. It folds closed, and folded it is low enough to build a bed over.

## The idea

A removable plywood kit that locks into the floor rails already in the van. It borrows its ideas from fold-out SUV sleeping platforms such as the Trunk Mate BlitzBed: light slotted panels on piano hinges, supports that slot together, pins to set the height, and no tools.

- A **drawer box** behind the sliding door. Its drawers pull out sideways through the door, which makes an outdoor kitchen and storage that are easy to reach. The end panels are open frames and the drawers have low sides with a full-height front, to keep the weight down.
- **Water** in one or two 35 L containers with taps (47 × 42 × 24 cm). They stand in the rear drawer, strapped to its front, so pulling the drawer out brings the tap to you, and the containers lift out from above to refill.
- A **storage box** along the left wall, joined to the back of the drawer box with wing nuts. It suits a leisure battery or bedding.
- **Plywood bed beams** that drop into slots in the boxes and in a **rear support** standing in front of the lift. The beams run beside the fixed seat, not over it. The support's feet stand on the rails, and ring pins set their height to level the bed.
- A **deck in two halves** that folds up and is the bed base, as on the BlitzBed. In each half, a front panel covers the boxes and shorter panels are hinged behind it. The panels are slotted, which saves weight and lets the mattress breathe. The front panel of the wall-side half lifts like a lid to reach the storage box.
- **Cut-outs wherever the wood doesn't carry load.** The box end panels and dividers are open frames, and the storage box is open frame with a lip so things stay in. The beams, rear support, feet and drawer sides get oval lightening holes. Only the panel between the drawers and the storage box stays solid, so nothing spills into the drawers.
- **It folds for driving.** The rear panels fold onto the front ones, the beams and support lie flat on top, and the mattress folds in three over them. The boxes stay closed and the floor behind them is free.
- **No drilling and no tools.** The boxes and the support's feet are held in the existing rails by seat-rated quick-release fittings, the same type the seats already use. The wheelchair conversion stays as it was approved, and the kit comes out in pieces one person can carry when the van is needed for the wheelchair.

## Using the planner

The planner is a single web page. Type in the van's measurements and it redraws everything to scale:

- a **3D view** you can turn and zoom, with the drawers in or out, the kit folded for driving, and a choice of showing everything, the slotted deck without the mattress, or just the frame and boxes
- a **plan**, a **side view** and a **cross-section** through the drawers
- **checks** that flag problems: a mattress that doesn't fit, too little headroom, drawers that are too long, water containers that don't fit, the seat in the way, a support foot with no rail clear of the seat and wheel arches, missing anchor points
- a **cut list** and a **cutting plan**: every part laid out on 2440 × 1220 boards (or whatever size you buy), with its cut-outs, a numbered cut order and a written cut-by-cut list for each board
- a **hardware list**, a **weight estimate**, and the weight of each piece you have to lift

The values it opens with are **examples**, taken from brochure figures and typical conversions. Replace them with real measurements. Every length is measured back from the front seats.

The measurements that matter most, in order:

1. **The seat's height when closed.** It sets the bed height, and the bed height decides whether you can sit up in bed.
2. **Floor to ceiling** above the bed area.
3. **The folded lift's depth** inside the van, including arms, handles and the hand pump.
4. **Floor length** from the back of the front seats to the rear doors.
5. **Where the rails are**, measured from the door-side wall, plus the make of the seats' quick-release fittings so matching ones can be bought.
6. **The sliding door opening**: where it starts, how wide it is, and how far the closed door reaches inside.
7. **The wheel arches**: where they start, how long and how high.
8. **The seat unfolded**: how far forward its backrest and cushion reach, if it should be usable with the bed folded away.

**Copy measurements** puts all values on the clipboard as plain text, ready to send to someone. Measurements are saved only in your own browser and are never uploaded.

## What the example numbers show

With the example values (high roof, seat about 48 cm high when closed, bed raised 5 cm above it, 10 cm mattress):

- A standard **200 × 140 cm** mattress fits, but on the short (L1) van only just: there's about 6.5 cm to spare in front of the lift. The folded lift's depth decides it. Up to about 46 cm deep it fits; any deeper and the mattress needs to be shorter. On the long (L2) van there's over 40 cm to spare.
- **Headroom is fine thanks to the high roof.** There's about 1 m from the mattress to the ceiling, so you can sit up in bed. Most adults need 85–90 cm.
- Two 1 m drawers fit under the bed, with low 25 cm sides and fronts about 48 cm high. The rear one holds **both 35 L water containers** standing up; the front one is the kitchen.
- A storage box about 57 cm deep sits behind the drawers.
- Four 12 cm deep plywood beams carry the bed from the boxes to one rear support, so nothing has to stand next to the seat.
- When pulled out, the drawer top is about 1.08 m off the ground, a bit above a kitchen worktop (90 cm).
- Folded for driving, the stack on the boxes reaches about 95 cm off the floor, mattress included.
- The 18 mm parts, beams and support included, fit on **four 2440 × 1220 boards** of 18 mm poplar (about 82% used). The drawers need one 12 mm board and one 9 mm board. Birch beams are stiffer but need a fifth 18 mm board of their own.
- The kit weighs about 95 kg in 18 mm poplar, mattress, slides and fittings included, or about 90 kg with 15 mm poplar boxes. The weight counts only the wood left after the cut-outs. The aluminium-frame version weighed about 118 kg. The heaviest piece is the drawer box with its drawers out, at about 23 kg; everything else is 12 kg or less.
- Add about 73 kg of water when full. It sits low and between the axles, which is the right place for it, but the water drawer needs slides rated around 140 kg.

## Building it safely

- **Anchor only to the rails**, with seat-rated quick-release fittings of the same type the seats use. Seat anchorages are tested at 20 g, so even a 70 kg kit can push forward with more than a tonne in a crash.
- **Use locking drawer slides**: they lock shut for driving and lock open on a slope while cooking.
- **Strap the water containers down** to the front of their drawer. Full, each one is a 36 kg block, and the drawer sides are low.
- **Strap the folded kit down for driving.** The loose beams, support and folded deck sit on the boxes, so hold them with ratchet straps to rail tie-down rings, not light cord.
- **Keep the lift usable.** Its hand pump and emergency release must stay reachable with the bed in place.
- **Cook outside**, on the pulled-out drawer.
- **Ventilate under the mattress.** The deck slots do most of this; add an anti-condensation mat in cold weather to keep mould away.
- **Check the paperwork.** A removable kit held by the existing rails is normally treated as secured load. A permanent motorhome conversion needs approval from the vehicle authority.
