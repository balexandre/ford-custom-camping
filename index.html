Transit Custom Camp Kit

A removable bed and side-door drawer kitchen for a van whose back is taken by a wheelchair lift. The bed sits over the folded seat, the drawers slide out through the sliding door, and everything bolts to the floor rails that are already there. Nothing gets drilled, and the van goes back to wheelchair use when the kit comes out.

This van

A high-roof Kombi with the first-generation body, built in 2023. One pair of floor rails runs under the left-hand seats and another runs down the middle for the wheelchair. The two left-hand seats sit on quick-release bases and come out for camping. The fold-up seat on the right, behind the sliding door, stays. A two-pillar platform lift folds up inside the rear doors.

    Hardware

      Measure these first

      1. Seat height when closed. It sets the bed height, and the bed height decides whether you can sit up.
      2. Floor to ceiling above where the bed will be.
      3. Folded lift depth, including its arms, handles and the hand pump.
      4. Floor length from the back of the front seats to the rear doors.
      5. Rail centres from the door-side wall, plus the make of the seats' quick-release fittings, so you can buy matching ones.
      6. Sliding door opening: where it starts, how wide it is, and how far the closed door reaches inside.
      7. Wheel arches: where they start, how long and how high.

      Build notes

      • Anchor to the rails only. Use seat-rated quick-release fittings of the same type the seats already use. Don't drill the floor: the rails and floor are part of the approved wheelchair conversion. Seat anchorages are tested at 20 g, so even a 70 kg kit can push forward with more than a tonne in a crash.
      • Use locking slides. Lock-in keeps the drawers shut on the road; lock-out keeps them open on a slope while you cook.
      • Keep the lift usable. Its hand pump and emergency release must stay reachable with the bed in place.
      • Cook outside on the pulled-out drawer, never under the bed deck with the door shut.
      • Ventilate the mattress. Drill 35 mm holes on a 150 mm grid in the deck or use an anti-condensation mat, or mould will grow under it.
      • Build in modules (drawer cabinet, aluminium frame, deck panels) so two people can take the kit out. Lightening holes in the big cabinet panels save several kilos.
      • Paperwork. A removable kit held by the existing rails is normally treated as secured load. A permanent motorhome conversion needs approval, so check with your vehicle authority before fixing anything permanently.
