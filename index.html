Transit Custom Camp Kit

A removable plywood bed and side-door drawer kitchen for a van whose back is taken by a wheelchair lift. Two boxes hold the drawers and storage, a slotted deck on piano hinges folds out over the folded seat onto a slot-together support, and everything locks to the floor rails that are already there. Nothing gets drilled, it goes together without tools, and it comes out in pieces one person can carry.

This van

A high-roof Kombi with the first-generation body, built in 2023. One pair of floor rails runs under the left-hand seats and another runs down the middle for the wheelchair. The two left-hand seats sit on quick-release bases and come out for camping. The fold-up seat on the right, behind the sliding door, stays. A two-pillar platform lift folds up inside the rear doors.

    Cutting plan

    Every part laid out on the boards. Each cut runs straight across the piece you're holding, so a track saw or a circular saw with a straight edge can do all of it. Follow the numbers on each board, or open "Cut by cut", for an order that works. Distances run from the top edge or left end of each piece as drawn, so keep the blade on the waste side of the line. Long strips keep their length along the board, with the grain, and the hatched areas are offcuts.

    Hardware

      Measure these first

      1. Seat height when closed. It sets the bed height, and the bed height decides whether you can sit up.
      2. Floor to ceiling above where the bed will be.
      3. Folded lift depth, including its arms, handles and the hand pump.
      4. Floor length from the back of the front seats to the rear doors.
      5. Rail centres from the door-side wall, plus the make of the seats' quick-release fittings, so you can buy matching ones.
      6. Sliding door opening: where it starts, how wide it is, and how far the closed door reaches inside.
      7. Wheel arches: where they start, how long and how high.
      8. The seat unfolded: how far forward its backrest and cushion reach, if you want to use it with the bed folded away.

      Build notes

      • Anchor to the rails only. Use seat-rated quick-release fittings of the same type the seats already use. Don't drill the floor: the rails and floor are part of the approved wheelchair conversion. Seat anchorages are tested at 20 g, so even a 70 kg kit can push forward with more than a tonne in a crash.
      • Use locking slides. Lock-in keeps the drawers shut on the road; lock-out keeps them open on a slope while you cook.
      • Keep the lift usable. Its hand pump and emergency release must stay reachable with the bed in place.
      • Cook outside on the pulled-out drawer, never under the bed deck with the door shut.
      • Slot the deck. Cut 30 × 200 mm slots between the beams, keeping 60 mm solid over each beam and along the edges. They save weight and let the mattress breathe. Add an anti-condensation mat in cold weather.
      • No tools to set up. The bed beams drop into slots in the boxes and the rear support. The storage box joins the drawer box with wing nuts, and ring pins set the height of the support's feet in 25 mm steps, to level the bed or fit a deeper lift.
      • Fold it for driving. In each deck half, the front panel covers the boxes and the rear panels fold onto it like a Z: put the hinge between the front panel and the next one on top, and the next hinge underneath. Lay the support and beams flat on the folded panels, fold the mattress over them and strap the stack to the rails. The boxes stay closed, and the floor behind them is free.
      • The storage lid. The front panel of the wall-side half lifts at its front edge like a lid, pivoting on its hinge, to reach the storage box.
      • Lift it out in pieces. Take the drawers out before lifting the drawer box. Every other piece is light enough for one person.
      • Paperwork. A removable kit held by the existing rails is normally treated as secured load. A permanent motorhome conversion needs approval, so check with your vehicle authority before fixing anything permanently.
